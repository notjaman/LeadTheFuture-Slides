The fix

One app for the whole day

Nothing to download, nothing to install. Log in and your phone has a conference app; open it on a laptop and it’s a website. Want it handier? Add it to your home screen — a real icon that opens straight in, with no browser bar.

During · Materials

Materials unlock as
each session starts

Every handout is ready in advance, but it stays locked. The moment that session begins, it opens — so nobody reads ahead, and nobody chases a speaker for a file afterwards.

Before the talk

It’s already loaded, just not open yet. The whole room moves together.

Opens on cue

The session starts, its materials appear on your page. Refresh and they’re there.

Yours only

Not a public link. You need to be logged in, so forwarding it to someone gets them nowhere.

It doesn’t vanish when the event ends — everything stays in your account.

During · 2

Q&A: no hands,
no interrupting

Type your question into your phone. You can see everyone else’s too, and upvote the ones you want answered.

The host sees a list sorted by votes — so the questions asked are the ones the room wants, not whoever’s hand went up first.

Questions show initials only — never full names.

During · 3

Ten questions,
two minutes, one course

The last piece is course matching. Ten questions, all about how you actually work — where your time goes, what you’re stuck on, what you want to build.

At the end you get the course that fits, plus why that one.

What I took away

Three things I learned

01

The hard part wasn’t technical. It was hesitation.

People who don’t raise a hand aren’t people without questions. They just don’t want to interrupt. Give them a way in that costs nothing socially, and the questions show up on their own.

02

The fastest counter is the one nobody queues at

Same QR code, opposite direction — staff scanning you became you scanning a sign — and the line was gone. Which way a thing points matters more than what it can do.

03

If you can explain it, you can build it

What saved the time was knowing exactly where a registration desk jams up. The rest was describing it clearly — which is more or less what this whole conference is about.

One last thing
