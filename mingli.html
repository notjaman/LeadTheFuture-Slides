Before and during · getting the materials

Every handout is ready in advance, it is just locked. Once that session starts, it unlocks by itself. Nothing to hunt for early, nobody to chase afterwards.

Before the talk

During · 2

No hands needed.
The room votes questions up

Type your question into your phone. You can see everyone else’s and upvote the good ones.

The host takes the most-upvoted questions first, so the room hears what it wants.

Initials only, never full names. The Chinese and English days have separate Q&A.

Three pages: Materials, where each session’s files stay locked until that session starts.

Questions, where anyone can post a question and upvote others, sorted by votes.

Course Match, a ten-question quiz that recommends one course and says why.

No spec, no wireframes, no mockups. It started from this paragraph.

What I took away

Three things I learned

01

The hard part wasn’t technical. It was hesitation.

Some people don’t raise a hand simply because they don’t want to interrupt. Give them a way that needs no hand, and the questions show up on their own.

02

Letting people do it themselves beats making them queue

Same QR code, just pointed the other way. Staff scanning you became you scanning the sign at the door, and the queue was gone. How a flow is designed matters more than how many features it has.

03

Describe what you need clearly and it gets built

What actually saved time was knowing exactly where a registration desk jams up. Everything else only had to be described clearly, and the app got built. That is what today is about.
